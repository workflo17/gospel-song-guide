Two things that change the plan he has now

Both of them decide what he should do tonight, and both are easy to get backwards.

Neither app does the other's job

ACE‑Step writes the song and sings it in a voice that is not his. Applio takes that finished vocal and replaces the timbre with his. There is no "use my voice" setting in one and no "write me a song" setting in the other. The build is the handoff between them.

Voice conversion runs after the song exists, so the order never changes: ACE‑Step writes the track, Demucs pulls the vocal out, Applio makes that vocal his, ffmpeg puts it back over the instrumental. Every command is on the ACE‑Step and Applio page.

The download is not what is holding him up

Applio with no trained model does nothing. It needs a .pth and an .index file, and those come from ten to thirty minutes of him singing, sliced into clips and trained on a free Colab GPU.

So the recording session is the real gate, not the install. He can do it tonight while ACE‑Step is still coming down the wire.

Three songs, written this afternoon

Pull the three readings

gospel.py takes any date within thirty days and writes clean JSON. All three days came down in one pass:

Wednesday is the workers in the vineyard, Thursday the wedding banquet, Friday the greatest commandment. Three parables that do not sound alike, which is a good week to start on.

Why not the Vatican News page

It is a good page to read in the morning, but it is built for reading rather than for scripts. It serves today only, the readings sit inside the site furniture, and it carries no daily commentary. Evangelizo, the feed behind evangeliodeldia.org, returns all four readings plus a commentary by a Church Father or a saint in a single call, with no key and no signup.

That commentary is the reason to prefer it. It hands the song a specific angle, and a lyric written from the angle does not come out sounding like a summary of the passage.

3

Him, today · 20 minutes

Run the three prompts in Grok, one chat each

Not the Automation yet. Three separate chats, so Wednesday's vineyard does not bleed into Thursday's wedding feast. Paste one file, save the reply as letras/2026-08-19-letra.txt, start a new chat, repeat.

Before saving each one, check that all seven blocks came back and that the chorus is identical every time it appears. If Grok summarised the passage instead of writing from the commentary, say so and ask again. That correction costs nothing now and costs a rerender later.

4

Him, today · 10 minutes

Rewrite one line in each chorus

Every reply ends with ===UNA_LINEA_PARA_TI===, which names one line of the chorus for him to rewrite in his own words. Three lines across three songs.

This is not a formality. It is the human authorship that keeps the channel clear of YouTube's inauthentic content policy, and it is also the quickest way to stop the songs sounding like a model wrote them.

Part B · This week

Getting his voice onto the vocal

One long session that happens once, then seconds a day forever after.

5

Him, alone · 45 minutes

Record the singing dataset

Ten to thirty minutes of him singing, not talking. The model learns his timbre across his range, and a dataset of speech gives a thin, strained result the moment the chorus goes high.

Solo and dry: no backing track, no reverb, no autotune. If a backing track bleeds in, the model learns the backing track too. Cover the low verses and the high choruses, same mic, same room, one sitting. GarageBand is already on the Mac and is more than enough. Export with Share, Export Song to Disk, and choose WAV. 

$ cd ~/gospel-song && mkdir -p dataset
$ ffmpeg -i canto_crudo.wav -f segment -segment_time 10 \
    -ac 1 -ar 44100 -c:a pcm_s16le dataset/voz_%03d.wav

Then delete any clip that is silence, a cough, or a false start.

Check ls dataset | wc -l shows at least 60 files, and three at random are clean solo singing.
6

Screen share · 20 minutes, then an hour of waiting

Train the voice in Colab

Zip the dataset, put it on his Google Drive, and open the Applio Colab notebook from the Applio repository. Set Runtime, Change runtime type, T4 GPU before running any cell. Sample rate 40k, around 300 epochs, save every 50.

Do not let him train this on the Mac

RVC training does not properly use the Apple GPU. It falls back to the CPU silently, prints epochs, and never meaningfully progresses. People report six hours and more for a thirty minute dataset. This is the single most likely way for him to give up in week one.

Saving every 50 epochs matters because free Colab sessions get reclaimed. A disconnect at epoch 240 then costs one checkpoint instead of the whole run.

Check A .pth and an .index sit in ~/gospel-song/voz/, and the .pth is somewhere between 50 and 200 MB. A few kilobytes means training never finished.
7

Him · 5 minutes a song

Make the track in ACE-Step

Paste the ===LETRA=== block into the lyrics field and the ===ESTILO=== block into the tags field, then set the duration in seconds. The [verse] and [chorus] tags stay in English even though the words are Spanish, because that is what the model reads to structure the song. Losing them in a paste is the most common cause of a track that comes back shapeless.

Install and first launch take an evening, mostly downloading. That is all covered command by command on the ACE‑Step and Applio page.

8

Local · about 3 minutes a song

Split the vocal, convert it, mix it back

Demucs separates the sung vocal from everything else:

$ demucs --two-stems=vocals cancion.wav
# -> separated/htdemucs/cancion/{vocals.wav, no_vocals.wav}

Applio runs in a browser tab rather than on the Mac, so this part is an upload and a download. Open the Colab notebook, take the gradio.live address it prints, and drop the .pth and .index from step 6 into the Download tab. Then on Inference: upload vocals.wav, tick I agree to the terms of use, press Convert, and Export Audio. Move the download out of ~/Downloads and call it vocals_clonada.wav.

Then put it back over the instrumental:

$ ffmpeg -i separated/htdemucs/cancion/no_vocals.wav -i vocals_clonada.wav \
    -filter_complex "[1:a]volume=1.15[v];[0:a][v]amix=inputs=2:duration=longest" \
    -c:a pcm_s16le final.wav

The cheapest good decision he will make

Any spoken part, an intro line or the citation read aloud, he should record himself. It takes ninety seconds, it sounds better than any clone, and it is the strongest defence he has against the inauthentic content policy. Save the cloning for the singing, which he cannot perform daily.

Part C · This week

The video, with the reading on screen

A lyric video with a citation card gets him to the three to five minutes he wants.

9

Local · about a minute a song

Get word timings from the audio

Run faster-whisper over final.wav with word timestamps and write an .ass subtitle file. Because the exact lyrics are already known from step 3, this is forced alignment rather than transcription, so it lands accurately even on sung Spanish.

10

Local · a few minutes to render

Render it in one ffmpeg call

One still image with a slow push and the lyrics burned in on time. For the Gospel references he wants alongside the song, the cheapest version that works is a fifteen second opening card carrying the citation and a short line from the passage, then the lyrics for the rest. It reads clearly on a phone and adds nothing to the daily workload. 

$ ffmpeg -loop 1 -i fondo.jpg -i final.wav \
    -vf "scale=3840:-2,\
zoompan=z='min(zoom+0.00015,1.18)':d=25*215:\
x='iw/2-(iw/zoom/2)':y='ih/2-(ih/zoom/2)':s=1920x1080:fps=25,\
subtitles=letra.ass" \
    -c:v libx264 -preset medium -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
    -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest salida.mp4

d=25*215 is the pan length in frames, which is 25 fps times the song length in seconds. Read it from the actual audio duration. Hard-code it and the push finishes early, then the picture sits frozen for the last minute.

Check The MP4 plays end to end, the words land on the beat, and the citation is readable at phone size.

After two weeks by hand

Have a coding AI write run_dia.py

Prompt 2 on the Prompts page turns steps 7 through 10 into one script that reads the email, makes the track, converts the voice, cuts the video, and leaves an MP4 in salida/AAAA-MM-DD/. It deliberately stops there and never uploads.

The reason to wait is simple. Automating a process he has not performed yet produces a script that automates the wrong thing.

Three things that can cost him the channel

None of them are hard. All of them are much harder to fix retroactively.

A fully automatic channel is what the policy was written to catch

Mass-produced AI uploads earn a warning, then a 90 day suspension, then permanent removal from the Partner Program. What keeps him on the right side of it is small: a real spoken intro in his own voice and the one hand-edited chorus line, every day. That makes it AI-assisted work with human direction, which is explicitly allowed. Build the habit on day one, because adding it after a strike does not undo the strike.

The Spanish scripture text is a copyrighted translation

Lyrics inspired by the passage are his own work and are safe to publish and monetise. The translation set to music, word for word, is not. The prompt already tells Grok to paraphrase. Cite the passage in the description and keep the lyric original.

Every upload needs the synthetic content disclosure

YouTube requires it for altered or synthetic media, and the voice model puts him squarely in scope. It is a checkbox at upload time. Ticking it costs nothing. Skipping it puts the whole catalogue at risk over a formality.
