A pipeline that reads the day's Gospel from evangeliodeldia.org, writes a song from it, renders it in a cloned voice, cuts a 3 to 4 minute video, and puts it on YouTube. Free tools throughout, with the licence traps marked.

Source evangelizo.org feed Language Spanish (SP) Brain Grok Verified 15 Aug 2026

Read these two things before choosing any tool

Both of them quietly decide the whole build, and both are easy to get wrong three weeks in.

Voice cloning does not sing

Text-to-speech cloning (Chatterbox, XTTS, F5) produces speech. Feed it lyrics and you get a man reading lyrics aloud. Getting his voice to actually sing is a different family of tool called voice conversion (RVC), which takes an existing sung vocal and swaps the timbre.

So there are two builds, not one, and they need different training data.

Grok cannot run the pipeline

Grok Automations (launched 16 July 2026) can run a prompt on a schedule and email you the result. It has no local code execution, no third-party connectors, and no branching logic.

So Grok is the writer and the alarm clock. Something else has to be the hands: his PC, or a free GitHub Actions runner.

Consequence

The workflow splits in two halves at a mailbox. Grok wakes up, reads the Gospel, writes the lyrics, and emails them. A script picks the email up and does audio, video, and upload. Every design below follows from that split.

The pipeline, six stages

Each stage names the free pick, why it beats the alternatives, and the exact thing to run.

1

Get the day's Gospel

evangeliodeldia.org is the Spanish face of Evangelizo, and Evangelizo publishes an open feed with no key and no signup. It returns the liturgical title, the saint, all four readings with citations, and, most usefully, the day's commentary by a Church Father, saint, or pope.

That commentary is the reason to use this source over scraping a readings page. It hands you the day's theological angle already worked out, which is exactly what a lyric needs and what a generic paraphrase of the passage never has.

The one call that matters

https://feed.evangelizo.org/v2/reader.php?date=20260815&type=reading&lang=SP&content=GSP

type: reading, reading_st (citation), comment, comment_a (author), saint, liturgic_t, feast. content: FR first reading, PS psalm, SR second reading, GSP gospel. lang: SP, PT, AM, FR, IT, DE, PL, NL, AR.

Two limits, both real. Dates cannot be more than 30 days from today, so there is no backfilling a year of content. And it returns HTML fragments, not JSON, so the tags need stripping.

I wrote and tested a fetcher that handles both: gospel.py, which pulls all four readings plus the commentary and writes clean UTF-8 JSON. 

python gospel.py --out hoy.json
# 2026-08-15 [SP] -> hoy.json
#   gospel     Lc 1,39-56.  (1523 chars)
#   commentary Beato Columba Marmion (1858-1923), abad  (1403 chars)
2

Turn it into lyrics

This is Grok's job, and the prompt is the single highest-value piece of the build. It needs to produce more than a lyric: it needs the song structure with a target runtime, a style tag string for the music model, and the YouTube metadata, all in a fixed shape a script can parse.

Write original verse, do not set the translation to music

The Spanish text on evangeliodeldia.org comes from a modern translation that is under copyright. Lyrics inspired by the passage are his own work and are safe to publish and monetise. A verbatim translated passage sung over music is not. Tell Grok to paraphrase, never to quote, and cite the passage in the description instead.

The prompt itself lives on its own page

The full Spanish prompt, with every editable line marked and a copy button, is on the Prompts page, alongside two others: one that builds the automation script, and one for when something breaks. Keeping them in one place stops the version he pastes from drifting away from the version documented here.

The shape that matters: it returns fixed ===SECTION=== delimiters so a script can split the email with one regex, and the [verse] and [chorus] tags stay in English because that is what ACE-Step reads to structure the song, even though the lyrics themselves are Spanish.

In the Grok app: Automations, new task, schedule daily at 05:00 in his timezone, delivery by email. Automations need a paid tier, either X Premium+ or a grok.com subscription.

3

Clone the voice

Everything here is free and runs locally. The column that actually decides the pick is licence, because most of the best-sounding open models are non-commercial, and a monetised YouTube channel is commercial use.

Non-commercial licences are fine for a hobby channel with no ads. They stop being fine the day he joins the YouTube Partner Program.

The cheapest good answer

He should record the spoken parts in his actual voice. It takes ninety seconds, it sounds better than any clone, and it is the single strongest defence against YouTube's inauthentic-content policy (stage 6).

Save the cloning for the sung parts, which he cannot perform daily. That is the job RVC exists for.

Training the singing model, once

  1. Record 10 to 30 minutes of him singing, solo, dry, no reverb, no backing track. Singing matters: the model learns timbre across his pitch range, and a speech-only dataset gives a thin, strained result on high notes.
  2. Slice into 5 to 15 second clips, 44.1 kHz mono WAV.
  3. Train in Applio (the maintained RVC fork with a real UI). A consumer GPU does it in a few hours; a free Colab session also works.
  4. Keep the .pth and .index pair. That is his voice, reusable forever.
4

Make the music

ACE-Step v1 3.5B is the pick, on licence alone: Apache 2.0, free for commercial use, weights on Hugging Face, and it runs natively in ComfyUI. It takes the lyrics and the style string from stage 2 and renders a full song with vocals. A four minute track takes about twenty seconds on an A100 and well under a minute on a consumer card.

Suno is easier and sounds better today. Its free tier grants non-commercial rights only, so the moment the channel monetises he either pays for a plan that grants commercial rights or he has a problem. Worth knowing before building a year of catalogue on it.

Getting his voice onto the sung vocal

  1. ACE-Step renders the song with a generic vocal.
  2. Demucs or UVR splits it into vocal stem and instrumental.
  3. RVC converts the vocal stem to his timbre using the model from stage 3.
  4. Mix the converted vocal back over the instrumental in ffmpeg or Audacity.
demucs --two-stems=vocals cancion.wav
# -> separated/htdemucs/cancion/{vocals.wav, no_vocals.wav}

# after RVC produces vocals_clonada.wav:
ffmpeg -i separated/htdemucs/cancion/no_vocals.wav -i vocals_clonada.wav \
  -filter_complex "[1:a]volume=1.15[v];[0:a][v]amix=inputs=2:duration=longest" \
  -c:a pcm_s16le final.wav
5

Cut the video

A lyric video is the right format here, and not as a compromise. Devotional viewers read along and sing along, the words are the content, and it costs nothing to render. One still image with a slow push, lyrics burned in on time.

Timings from the audio, not by hand

Run faster-whisper over the finished vocal with word timestamps, then write an .ass subtitle file. Because the exact lyrics are already known, this is forced alignment rather than transcription, so it lands accurately even on sung Spanish.

One ffmpeg command for the whole render

ffmpeg -loop 1 -i fondo.jpg -i final.wav \
  -vf "scale=3840:-2,\
zoompan=z='min(zoom+0.00015,1.18)':d=25*215:\
x='iw/2-(iw/zoom/2)':y='ih/2-(ih/zoom/2)':s=1920x1080:fps=25,\
subtitles=letra.ass" \
  -c:v libx264 -preset medium -crf 18 -pix_fmt yuv420p \
  -c:a aac -b:a 192k -shortest salida.mp4

d=25*215 is the pan duration in frames: 25 fps times the song length in seconds. Set it from the actual audio duration or the pan finishes early and freezes.

For the background: one hand-picked image reused with a colour grade per liturgical season beats a different AI image every day. It builds a recognisable channel look, and it removes a daily failure point.

6

Publish

Upload runs through the YouTube Data API, videos.insert. Since Google's June 2026 quota change, uploads draw from their own bucket at 100 per day rather than the old shared 10,000-unit pool that capped projects near six. Volume is not the constraint here.

The blocker nobody mentions until day one

Any API project created after 28 July 2020 that has not passed Google's audit has all its uploads locked to private. The upload succeeds, the video exists, and it cannot be made public. No error explains this.

Two ways through. Request the API compliance audit from Google and wait, or upload as private and publish by hand from YouTube Studio, which takes about twenty seconds a day and gives him a final look before it goes out. Start with the second one.

Be honest about this one

A fully hands-off daily AI song channel is precisely the profile the inauthentic-content policy was written to catch. Mass-produced AI output with no original input is not eligible for monetisation, and the enforcement ladder ends in permanent removal.

The fix is small and it is not optional. Every day he should record a short spoken intro in his real voice and change at least one line of the lyric himself. Two minutes of actual human work converts the video from mass-produced to AI-assisted with human creative direction, which YouTube explicitly permits with disclosure. Build the review step into the workflow from day one rather than bolting it on after a strike.

Two ways to wire it, and which to build first

Route A · Grok drives, he finishes

No code. Build it this week.

  • Grok Automation, daily 05:00, emails the lyrics block
  • He pastes the lyrics and style into ACE-Step
  • Picks the best of three takes, runs RVC
  • Drops audio into a saved video template
  • Uploads and publishes from Studio

About 15 minutes a day. Zero infrastructure, nothing to break, and it proves the format before anything gets automated.

Route B · Fully scripted

Once the format stops changing.

  • Windows Task Scheduler runs run_day.py at 05:00
  • gospel.py pulls the readings and commentary
  • xAI API (grok-4.6) writes the lyrics from the same prompt
  • ACE-Step, Demucs, RVC, ffmpeg run headless in sequence
  • Uploads as private, sends him one notification

About 2 minutes a day to review and publish. The xAI API costs a few cents a day at these token counts.

Order matters

Build Route A first even if he intends to end at Route B. The format will change five times in the first two weeks, and every change is free in Route A and a code edit in Route B. Automate the workflow once, after it has stopped moving.

Why not n8n, Zapier, or Make for the middle?

They handle the email trigger fine, and then they hit a wall: ACE-Step, Demucs, RVC, and ffmpeg all need a local GPU and a real filesystem. A cloud automation tool ends up doing nothing but calling a webhook on his PC, which a scheduled Python script does directly with one fewer service, one fewer account, and one fewer thing to expire.

n8n earns its place only if he later wants the same song pushed to Spotify, Instagram, and a mailing list at once. That is a distribution problem, not a production one.

Day one

In order, because each one de-risks the next.

  1. Open the feed URL in a browser with today's date. If Spanish text comes back, the hardest dependency is already solved and free forever.
  2. Set up the Grok Automation with the prompt above. Let it email him for three days before building anything else. Read the lyrics critically: this tells you whether the concept works at all.
  3. Record the voice dataset while step 2 runs. 10 to 30 minutes of solo singing, dry, one sitting. This is the long pole and it only has to happen once.
  4. Make one video entirely by hand, start to finish, no automation. It will take an hour and it will surface every decision the automated version has to make.
  5. Upload it as private and confirm whether the API audit lock applies to his project before writing any upload code.
  6. Only then automate, and only the steps that stopped changing.

What is already built and tested

gospel.py is working and verified against the live feed on 15 August 2026. It pulls all four readings with citations, the liturgical title, the saints, and the full daily commentary, and writes clean UTF-8 JSON. It falls back to the OS certificate store, which matters on any Windows machine running antivirus HTTPS scanning, where the normal certificate bundle fails. 

python gospel.py                      # today, Spanish, to stdout
python gospel.py --out hoy.json       # to a file
python gospel.py 2026-08-20 --lang PT # another date, Portuguese
python gospel.py --gospel-only        # skip the other readings

Everything else in this document is specified but not built, because it depends on his machine: whether he has a GPU, how much VRAM, and whether the channel will be monetised.
