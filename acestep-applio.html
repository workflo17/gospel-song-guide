Complete walkthrough · no experience assumed

Making the song

Start with the lyrics Grok wrote this morning. Finish with a full song sung in your own voice. Every button, every command, and what you should see after each one.

You need a Mac and a Google account One-time setup about 4 hours, spread over 2 evenings Then daily about 20 minutes Cost nothing

What you are about to do

Two free apps do the work. The first one, ACE-Step, reads your lyrics and produces a finished song with singing and instruments. The singer will be a stranger's voice. The second app, Applio, takes that singing and replaces the voice with yours.

ACE-Step gets installed on your Mac. Applio you use in a web browser, so there is nothing to install for it at all.

Parts 1, 2 and 3 you never have to do again. If you only have one evening, do part 1. Everything else waits, and part 1 is the piece that takes real effort from you rather than from the computer.

First, about Terminal

Some steps ask you to type a command. If you have never done that, read this once and you are set.

Terminal is an app already on your Mac. It is a window where you type instructions instead of clicking them. To open it: hold Command and press Space, type Terminal, and press Return.

When this page shows a grey box like the one below, copy the whole thing, click into the Terminal window, paste it, and press Return. Paste is Command and V, the same as anywhere else on the Mac.

A command box looks like this
cd ~/gospel-song

Terminal shows a lot of text while it works. You do not need to read it. What matters is whether the next step's check passes. Terminal also never asks "are you sure", so paste one box at a time and let each finish before the next.

Part 1: Record your voice Once, ever

This is the only part where your effort decides the result. Everything after is the computer's job.

1

Open GarageBand and set up a blank recording

5 minutesYou, alone

GarageBand is free and already on your Mac. You do not need to know how to use it.

  1. Hold Command, press Space, type GarageBand, press Return.
  2. A window asks what kind of project you want. Click Empty Project, then click Choose at the bottom.
  3. Now it asks what kind of track. Click the one with the microphone icon, called Audio. Then click Create.
  4. You will see an empty grey workspace with a red circle button near the top middle. That red circle is Record. Do not press it yet.
You should see

An empty project window with one track on the left and a red record button at the top.

2

Sing for 10 to 30 minutes

30 minutesYou, alone, in a quiet room

You are not performing. You are giving the computer material to learn from. Nobody hears this recording except the training program.

Sing, do not talk. This is the mistake that ruins the whole thing. A recording of you speaking teaches the computer your speaking voice, and then every high note in every song afterwards comes out thin and strained. Sing.

What to sing: anything you know well. Hymns you have sung for years are perfect, because you are not thinking about the words. Sing some low and slow, and some high and loud. Whatever part of your range you never record, the computer has to guess at later.

How to do it right:

  1. Turn off any music playing in the room. If you want a backing track to keep you in tune, use headphones. If the microphone hears the backing track, the computer learns the backing track too.
  2. Sit the same distance from the microphone the whole time. Your Mac's built-in microphone is fine. A cheap USB microphone is better. Either beats moving around.
  3. Press the red Record button and sing. Stop when you have done at least ten minutes. Thirty is better.
  4. Do it all in one sitting, in one room. Consistency matters more than quality here.
You should see

A long blue or green bar across the track, showing what you recorded. Play it back and listen. You should hear only yourself.

3

Save the recording where the Mac can find it

5 minutesYou, alone
  1. In GarageBand's top menu click Share, then Export Song to Disk.
  2. A box appears. Where it asks for the format, choose WAV. Not MP3. MP3 throws away detail the computer needs.
  3. Name it canto and click Export. Save it to your Desktop so it is easy to find.
  4. Now open Finder. Hold Command and Shift and press H. This opens your Home folder.
  5. Find the folder called gospel-song. Drag canto.wav from your Desktop into it.

If there is no gospel-song folder yet, paste this into Terminal to make one, then drag the file in:

mkdir -p ~/gospel-song
You should see

A file called canto.wav sitting inside the gospel-song folder in Finder.

4

Check whether your Mac already has the audio tool

1 minuteYou, alone

Cutting the recording up needs a free program called ffmpeg. Your Mac does not come with it. Before anything else, find out whether it is already there.

Open Terminal: hold Command, press Space, type Terminal, press Return. Then paste this line and press Return:

ffmpeg -version

You get one of exactly two answers, and they send you to different places:

What Terminal saysWhat it meansWhere to go
Lots of text starting ffmpeg version 7... You already have it Skip step 5 completely. Go straight to step 6.
command not found: ffmpeg Not installed yet Do step 5 first, then carry on.
5

Install the audio tool

20 to 30 minutes, mostly waitingOnly if step 4 said "command not found"

This is the longest one-time step on the page, and nearly all of it is your Mac downloading quietly. Four boxes, in order. Let each one finish before starting the next.

Box 1. Apple's developer tools. Nothing else installs without these.

xcode-select --install

A window pops up. Click Install and wait. If Terminal instead tells you it is already installed, that is a pass, move on.

Box 2. Homebrew, which is the thing that fetches ffmpeg for you.

/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"

It will ask for your Mac password. As you type, nothing appears on screen. No dots, no stars, nothing at all. That is normal and it is not broken. Type your password anyway and press Return.

Box 3. This is the one everybody skips, and skipping it makes everything afterwards look broken. Homebrew puts itself somewhere Terminal does not check by default. These two lines tell Terminal where to look.

echo 'eval "$(/opt/homebrew/bin/brew shellenv)"' >> ~/.zprofile eval "$(/opt/homebrew/bin/brew shellenv)"

Box 4. Finally ffmpeg itself. This is the slow one, roughly 10 to 20 minutes.

brew install ffmpeg
You should see

Paste ffmpeg -version again. This time it prints a wall of text instead of "command not found".

If it still says command not found, quit Terminal completely with Command and Q, not the red dot on the window, and open it again. Box 3 only takes effect in a fresh Terminal.

6

Make sure the Mac can find your recording

2 minutesYou, alone

The next step looks for a file called exactly canto.wav inside your gospel-song folder. Check it is really there before running anything.

ls ~/gospel-song

That lists what is in the folder. You want canto.wav in the list.

What you seeWhat to do about it
canto.wav is listed Good. Go to step 7.
No such file or directory The folder does not exist yet. Paste mkdir -p ~/gospel-song, drag your recording into it, then try again.
A different name, like canto 1.wav Rename it. In Finder click the file once, press Return, type canto.wav, press Return again.
canto.wav.wav You typed the ending twice, because Finder was hiding it. Rename it to just canto and Finder keeps the real ending.

Finder hides file endings by default, so something showing as canto in Finder is usually canto.wav underneath. ls always shows the real full name, which is why this step uses it rather than your eyes.

7

Cut the recording into small pieces

2 minutesYou, alone

The training program cannot use one long recording. It needs many short ones. This chops yours into ten second pieces automatically. None of it is done by hand.

Paste all three lines at once
cd ~/gospel-song mkdir -p dataset ffmpeg -i canto.wav -f segment -segment_time 10 -ac 1 -ar 44100 -c:a pcm_s16le dataset/voz_%03d.wav

Text scrolls past for a few seconds. When it stops and you get a fresh line with a blinking cursor, it has finished. You do not need to read any of it.

Now count what you got:

ls ~/gospel-song/dataset | wc -l

That prints one number: how many pieces were made. Ten minutes of singing gives about 60. Thirty minutes gives about 180.

You should see

A number of 60 or higher. If it says 5, the recording was too short and you need to sing more.

8

Listen through and throw away the bad pieces

10 minutesYou, alone

This opens the folder in an ordinary Finder window, so you do not have to go hunting for it:

open ~/gospel-song/dataset

Then, in that Finder window:

  1. Press Command and 2 for list view, so you can see them all at once.
  2. Click the first file once. Do not double click, that opens a music app you do not need.
  3. Press the Space bar. The clip plays in a small preview window.
  4. Press the down arrow for the next file. It keeps playing each one as you move, so you can go through them fast.
  5. When you hit a bad one, press Command and Delete to bin it, then carry on down.
  6. Press Space again to close the preview when you are finished.

A bad one is silence, a cough, throat clearing, a false start, or the very last piece if it cuts off mid word. You do not have to check every file. Ten minutes of skimming is plenty.

You should see

At least 60 files still there after deleting, named voz_001.wav, voz_002.wav and so on. Three picked at random are you singing clearly, with no background noise and no long silence.

If deleting left you under 60, the recording had too much dead air in it. Record another ten minutes and run step 7 again. More good material always beats less.

Part 2: Teach the computer your voice Once, ever

This happens on Google's computers, not yours, and it is free.

Do not try to do this part on your Mac

Applio can technically train on a Mac and it will look like it is working. It prints progress, it never finishes. People wait six hours and get nothing. Google lends you a much faster machine for free, and it takes about an hour. Use Google's.

9

Put your recordings on Google Drive

10 minutesYou, alone
  1. In Finder, right-click the dataset folder and choose Compress "dataset". This makes a single file called dataset.zip.
  2. Go to drive.google.com in your browser and sign in.
  3. Drag dataset.zip from Finder onto the Google Drive page. Wait for it to finish uploading.
You should see

dataset.zip listed in your Google Drive.

10

Start the training

20 minutes of clicking, then an hour of waitingYou, alone

Open this address in your browser. Save it as a bookmark, because you will use it every day later.

https://colab.research.google.com/github/iahispano/applio/blob/main/assets/Applio.ipynb

What opens is a page full of grey blocks with small round play buttons on the left of each one. You click them from top to bottom, one at a time, and wait for each to finish before clicking the next. A green tick appears when a block is done.

  1. Before clicking anything, go to the Runtime menu at the top, choose Change runtime type, select T4 GPU, and click Save. Doing this after you start means starting over.
  2. Click the play button on the first block. Wait for the green tick. Move to the next. Keep going until every block has run.
  3. The last block prints a web address ending in gradio.live. Click it. Applio opens in a new tab.
  4. In Applio, click the Training tab along the top.
  5. Give your voice a name you will recognise in a year. Your own name is fine. Do not call it "test".
  6. Point it at your dataset.zip, set sample rate to 40k, epochs to 300, and save every 50.
  7. Start it, then leave the tab open and check back in an hour.

Google takes the free machine back if you ignore the tab for too long, so look at it every fifteen minutes or so. Saving every 50 epochs means an interruption costs you a few minutes, not the whole hour.

11

Download your voice and keep it safe

5 minutesYou, alone

When training finishes you get two files. One ends in .pth and one ends in .index. Those two files are your voice. They are the whole point of the last two hours.

  1. Download both to your Mac.
  2. In Finder, make a folder called voz inside gospel-song, and put both files there.
  3. Copy them somewhere else as well, a Google Drive folder or a USB stick. If you lose them you have to sing for thirty minutes again.
You should see

Two files in gospel-song/voz. Click the .pth file once and look at its size: it should be somewhere between 50 MB and 200 MB. If it is only a few kilobytes, the training did not really finish and you need to run it again.

Part 3: Install ACE-Step Once, ever

Four boxes to paste. Most of the time is the Mac downloading in the background.

12

Install the helper, then restart Terminal

5 minutesYou, alone

ACE-Step needs a small helper program called uv, which fetches everything else for it. One box:

Paste this into Terminal
curl -LsSf https://astral.sh/uv/install.sh | sh

Now quit Terminal completely and open it again. Press Command and Q, not just the red dot on the window. Terminal only notices new programs when it starts up, so if you skip this the next step fails and looks like the install broke when it did not.

You should see

In the new Terminal window, paste uv --version. It prints a number. If it says "command not found", you did not really quit Terminal.

13

Download ACE-Step

10 to 15 minutes, mostly waitingYou, alone Paste this into Terminal
cd ~ git clone https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5.git cd ACE-Step-1.5 uv sync

The last line is the slow one. A great deal of text scrolls past. Leave it alone until you get a fresh empty line back.

You should see

Paste uv run acestep --help. It prints a page of help text rather than a red error.

14

Start it for the first time

5 minutes, then up to 40 of downloadingYou, alone Paste this into Terminal
cd ~/ACE-Step-1.5 chmod +x start_gradio_ui_macos.sh ./start_gradio_ui_macos.sh

The very first time, it downloads the model that actually writes the music. That is between 4 and 9 GB, so it takes a while. It only happens once. Every day after this, the same command starts it in under a minute.

When it has finished, open your browser and go to:

http://localhost:7860

Leave the Terminal window open while you use ACE-Step. That window is the app running. Closing it switches the app off and the browser page stops working.

You should see

A page with a large box for lyrics and another box for style or tags.

Part 4: Turn today's lyrics into a song Every day

15

Yes, you copy and paste straight from Grok

3 minutesYou, alone

Grok's reply is one block of text with headings in it. You need two pieces out of it. A reply looks like this:

===TITULO=== Vengan tambien a mi vina ===ESTILO=== latin worship ballad, acoustic guitar, male vocal, warm pads, 72 bpm ===LETRA=== [verse] Salio de madrugada a buscar ... [chorus] ...

Do not copy the lines with equals signs. Those are only labels telling you where each piece starts. Copy the text underneath them.

Do keep the words in square brackets. [verse] and [chorus] get pasted along with the lyrics. They are instructions to the music program telling it where the verses and the chorus go. Delete those and the song comes out shapeless.

So, concretely:

  1. Copy the single line underneath ===ESTILO===. That is the style.
  2. Copy everything underneath ===LETRA===, starting at [verse] and stopping when you reach the next line with equals signs. That is the lyrics.

The style line is in English on purpose, even though the song is in Spanish. The music program only understands these descriptions in English. The lyrics stay in Spanish.

16

Make the song

5 minutesYou, alone

Start ACE-Step if it is not already running, then go to http://localhost:7860.

Paste this into Terminal to start it
cd ~/ACE-Step-1.5 ./start_gradio_ui_macos.sh
  1. Paste the style into the tags or style box.
  2. Paste the lyrics into the lyrics box.
  3. Find the length setting and set it to 210 seconds, which is three and a half minutes.
  4. Click Generate and wait. On most Macs this is one to three minutes.
  5. When the song appears, click the small download arrow on the player to save it.
  6. In Finder, move the downloaded file into gospel-song and rename it cancion.wav.

Keep the name simple. No spaces, no accents, no apostrophes. Later steps refuse awkward filenames and the error message does not tell you that is the problem.

You should see

cancion.wav in your gospel-song folder. Play it. It is a real song with your words, sung by a stranger. Fixing that is part 5.

Part 5: Put your voice on it Every day

17

Separate the singing from the instruments

2 minutesYou, alone

You cannot swap the voice while the instruments are mixed in with it. This command pulls them apart into two separate files.

Paste this into Terminal
cd ~/gospel-song demucs --two-stems=vocals cancion.wav
You should see

A new folder path separated/htdemucs/cancion/ containing vocals.wav, which is just the singing, and no_vocals.wav, which is just the music.

18

Open Applio and load your voice

5 minutesYou, alone, every day

This is the same bookmark from part 2. Applio does not stay open overnight, so you start it fresh each day.

  1. Open the bookmarked Colab page.
  2. Runtime, Change runtime type, T4 GPU, Save.
  3. Click the play buttons top to bottom, waiting for each green tick.
  4. Click the gradio.live link the last block prints.
  5. In Applio, click the Download tab, find the Drop files area, and drag in your .pth file, then your .index file.
  6. Click the Inference tab, then click Refresh. Your voice now appears in the Voice Model list.

The gradio.live address is different every single day, so do not bookmark it. The Colab page is the bookmark. Dropping your two voice files in again each day is normal and takes about thirty seconds.

You should see

Your voice name in the Voice Model dropdown, and its matching file in Index File.

19

Convert the singing to your voice

5 minutesYou, alone
  1. Choose your voice in Voice Model and its match in Index File.
  2. Under Upload Audio, drag in vocals.wav from the folder step 17 made. Or click the box and browse to it.
  3. Tick the box that says I agree to the terms of use.
  4. Click Convert and wait a few seconds.
  5. Click Export Audio to download the result.
  6. In Finder, move it from Downloads into gospel-song and rename it vocals_clonada.wav.

If Convert seems to do nothing, it is the terms box. Applio will not convert until that checkbox is ticked, and it gives you no message explaining why. This catches almost everybody on their first try.

You should see

Play the downloaded file. It is the same tune, same words, but now it is you singing.

20

Put the song back together

1 minuteYou, alone

Your voice and the instruments are two separate files. One command joins them.

Paste this into Terminal
cd ~/gospel-song ffmpeg -i separated/htdemucs/cancion/no_vocals.wav -i vocals_clonada.wav -filter_complex "[1:a]volume=1.15[v];[0:a][v]amix=inputs=2:duration=longest" -c:a pcm_s16le final.wav
You should see

final.wav in your gospel-song folder. That is the finished song, in your voice, ready for the video.

If your voice is too quiet against the music, change 1.15 to 1.3 and run it again. Too loud, try 1.0. Once it sounds right, it stays right for every song after.

Your day, once everything is set up

This is all that is left after the one-time parts are done.

  1. Open the Grok email. Copy the style line and the lyrics.
  2. Start ACE-Step in Terminal, open localhost:7860, paste both, set 210 seconds, Generate, download as cancion.wav.
  3. Run the demucs command.
  4. Start the Applio Colab page, drop in your two voice files, upload vocals.wav, tick the terms box, Convert, Export, rename to vocals_clonada.wav.
  5. Run the ffmpeg command. You now have final.wav.
  6. Rewrite one line of the chorus in your own words before you publish. This one matters and takes two minutes.

When something goes wrong

Find your symptom in the left column. Almost everything is one of these.

What you seeWhat it meansWhat to do
Convert does nothing in Applio The terms box is not ticked Tick I agree to the terms of use. There is no error message for this.
The Applio page will not load Yesterday's link is dead Open the Colab bookmark, run the blocks again, use the new link. Links last a day or two at most.
Your voice is missing from the list The files did not load, or the list is stale Click Refresh first. If still missing, drop the two files into the Download tab again.
"command not found" in Terminal Terminal has not restarted since the install Press Command and Q to quit Terminal fully, then open it again.
An error mentioning your audio file The filename has spaces or accents Rename it to plain letters with no spaces, then try again.
The song has no chorus or repeats oddly The bracket tags were lost Check [verse] and [chorus] are still in the lyrics box.
Your voice sounds tinny or robotic Usually the index rate In Applio's Advanced Settings lower the index rate. If every song does it, your recording was speech rather than singing.
Your voice is an octave too high or low The pitch setting In Advanced Settings set pitch to 12 or -12.
The browser page stopped responding You closed the Terminal window That window was the app. Start it again with the command from step 16.
